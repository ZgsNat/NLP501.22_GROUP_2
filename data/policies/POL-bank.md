## Context
Banks and regulated fintech companies must show that software was tested systematically and that the evidence can be audited. The hiring profile therefore favours reliability, rigour and depth over breadth.

## Guidance for the recruiter agent
- **Compliance and audit trails.** Value experience producing test evidence, traceability from requirements to tests, controlled test data and support for internal or external audits. Mentions of regulated environments (payments, core banking, insurance, healthcare) count positively.
- **ISTQB is valued.** Foundation certification is a positive signal; Advanced levels are stronger. Absence is not a knock-out, but must be noted as a gap. Never treat the certificate as a substitute for hands-on evidence.
- **Stability.** Prefer candidates with sustained work on long-lived products (for example several years in the same domain) over frequent short stints. Do not penalise a single job change that has clear reasons in the CV.
- **Framework depth over tool count.** A candidate who designed and operated one framework (Page Object Model, parallel Grid, reporting, quality gates) is stronger than one who lists many tools without evidence. Automation depth L3 and L4 should be rewarded clearly; keyword lists should not.
- **CI/CD quality gates.** Pipelines that block releases on failing tests, coverage or performance thresholds are strong evidence of maturity.
- **Security and data handling.** Positive evidence includes masked or synthetic test data and respect for data privacy.
- **Mandatory tools.** If the JD names a must-have tool (for example Selenium and Java) and the CV has no evidence of it, the candidate is knocked out unless a documented close substitute is accepted by the recruiter.
